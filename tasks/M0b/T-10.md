# T-10 — Atmosphere: sky, sun, clouds, fog and colour grading
ID / milestone / wave / risk: T-10 / M0b / wave 1 / medium (performance)

## Goal and why
Most of a voxel world's beauty comes from light and air. Make the daytime look beautiful and slightly melancholic, and cheap on the Vega 8 with the Compatibility renderer. Day/night comes in M3, so build the structure now: one `time_of_day` value drives the colours.

## Read
CLAUDE.md, docs/CONVENTIONS.md, docs/DECISIONS.md D-019 and D-020, world/environment.tscn.

## Interfaces / invariants
- `world/environment.tscn`, with a new script `world/atmosphere.gd` on its root:
  - `@export var time_of_day: float = 0.38` (0..1, where 0.5 is noon);
  - `func apply_time(t: float) -> void` sets the sun angle, sun colour/energy, sky colours, fog colour and ambient light from a small const gradient table. Keyframes: dawn 0.25, day 0.4–0.6, dusk 0.75, night 0.9. Night must be very dark blue, with the fog closer.
- Sky: `ProceduralSkyMaterial` (it works in Compatibility), with tuned sky_top, sky_horizon, ground_horizon and sun disc size. The fog colour equals the sky horizon colour, so distant terrain melts into the sky instead of meeting a grey wall.
- Fog: depth fog tuned so the last loaded chunks (radius 4, about 64 m) fade fully. Fog starts around 24 m and is fully opaque at about 60 m. Use the Compatibility-supported fog settings (check which `Environment` fog properties the Compatibility renderer honours in Godot 4.7 and document them in your report).
- Clouds: `world/clouds.tscn` + `world/clouds.gdshader`. One large horizontal plane (about 600×600 m) at y = 140 that follows the camera in x/z. The shader is unshaded and alpha-blended, with 2-octave value noise built in, a slow drift (wind), soft blocky edges (quantise noise UV to 8 m cells), coverage about 40%, and fade toward the far edges. It must stay cheap: no loops beyond 2 octaves, no texture reads except an optional 64×64 noise texture.
- Tonemapping: filmic or ACES (whichever Compatibility supports) with a slight contrast/saturation adjustment, if Compatibility supports `adjustment_*`. Otherwise skip it and say so.
- DirectionalLight3D: warm sun, shadows OFF (performance gate), energy about 1.0–1.2. Ambient light from the sky colour, energy about 0.5.
- Ambient sound is not in this task.

## Steps
1. Implement the above.
2. Unit test `tests/unit/test_atmosphere.gd`:
   - `apply_time(0.5)` gives a brighter sun energy than `apply_time(0.9)`;
   - fog colour equals sky horizon colour at 3 times of day;
   - the clouds scene instances.
3. Render check: the screenshot tool may not exist yet. Run Godot under `xvfb-run -a -s "-screen 0 1280x720x24"` with `--rendering-method gl_compatibility` on a tiny scene of your own: environment, clouds, a few BoxMeshes, a camera. Save 3 PNGs (times 0.3, 0.5, 0.75) under builds/, view them with the Read tool and iterate until it looks good. Put the paths in your report.

2. The 3 rendered PNGs exist and look right; describe them in your report.
3. No real-time shadows, no SSAO/SSR/glow, and no volumetric fog (all are absent or unsupported in Compatibility, or too expensive). State this in the report.
