# T-08 — Procedural pixel-art block atlas
ID / milestone / wave / risk: T-08 / M0b / wave 1 / medium (sets the art style)

## Goal and why
The owner wants a beautiful world. We generate our own cohesive 16×16 pixel-art atlas (D-021) in a Minecraft-like style, but with a more refined, natural and slightly moody palette. It must look hand-made, not like noise.

## Read
CLAUDE.md, docs/CONVENTIONS.md, docs/PLAN.md section "M0b visual contracts", docs/DECISIONS.md D-020 and D-021.

## Interfaces / invariants
- Output: `assets/textures/blocks_atlas.png` and `assets/textures/blocks_atlas.json`, exactly per the PLAN contract. The texture names below are the contract that T-09 uses.
- `tools/gen_textures.py` (python3 + Pillow; dev only). Deterministic: a fixed `random.Random(seed)` per texture name, so the same output every run. It also writes `builds/atlas_preview.png`: every tile scaled 6× with nearest neighbour, labelled, plus a 4×4 tiling preview of grass_top, stone, sand, dirt and water to show seams.
- **Required textures:** grass_top, grass_side (dirt with a ragged 3–4 px grass fringe at the top edge, varying per column), dirt, stone, cobblestone, gravel, sand, sandstone_top, sandstone_side, clay, snow, grass_side_snowed, log_oak_side, log_oak_top (rings), planks_oak, leaves_oak (alpha cut-outs, about 25% transparent pixels in clumps), log_birch_side, log_birch_top, leaves_birch, log_spruce_side, log_spruce_top, leaves_spruce, water (semi-opaque: alpha 200), tall_grass (cross sprite, alpha), fern, flower_red, flower_yellow, flower_blue, flower_white, dead_bush, mushroom_brown, mushroom_red, coal_ore, iron_ore, mossy_cobblestone.
- **grass_top, the leaves and tall_grass/fern are authored in a light neutral green-grey luminance range,** because the engine multiplies them by a biome tint vertex colour (`tint:grass|foliage`). Also provide a `tints` key in the json: `{"grass":{"plains":"#7fb04a","forest":"#6a9e3f","mountain":"#8aa86a","beach":"#8fb85a"}, "foliage":{...similar}}`.

## Art direction (follow closely)
- Each material uses 4–6 hand-picked shades. **Hue-shift:** shadows are cooler and more saturated toward blue/purple, highlights warmer toward yellow. No pure black, no pure white, no fully saturated colours.
- Readable structure over noise:
  - stone: soft irregular slabs with 1-px darker cracks and a few lighter specks;
  - cobblestone: rounded stones with dark mortar outlines and a highlight top-left;
  - dirt: clumps plus a few pebbles;
  - sand: fine 2-tone grain with sparse darker specks;
  - planks: 4 horizontal boards with grain lines and nail dots;
  - log side: vertical bark ridges;
  - log top: concentric rings with a bark border.
- Every texture tiles seamlessly: wrap all noise and features toroidally.
- Light from the top-left (highlight on upper-left edges, shadow on lower-right) for cobble, ores and gravel.
- Ores: stone base plus 3–5 small mineral clusters (coal: dark grey-black, iron: peach-tan).
- Flowers: small (5–8 px tall), with a stem and leaves, readable at 16 px.
- Palette starting points (adjust for beauty):
  - stone #8a8a8f, #77777e, #66666e, #9b9a9e;
  - dirt #8a5f3c, #74502f, #9c6e46, #5e3f26;
  - sand #dccf9a, #cbbd86, #e8dcac;
  - water #3d6fb0 → #5a8fd0;
  - snow #eef3f8, #dbe5ef, #c9d6e6.

## Out of scope / files not to touch / permitted dependencies

## Acceptance criteria and verification
1. Running `python3 tools/gen_textures.py` twice gives byte-identical PNGs (compare with md5sum).
2. The atlas is 256×256 RGBA and contains every required name in the json.
3. Seam test: a script verifies, for each opaque texture, that its left/right and top/bottom edge pixel differences fall within the same range as interior neighbour differences (no visible seam). Print the results.
4. The preview PNG path and your iteration notes are in your report.
5. `bash scripts/check.sh` still exits 0.
